🎟House Parlays

Core tickets: at least 5 legs, one per game, and only rules that cashed 30 or more times when rebuilt on every day of the walk-forward season replay. Each game's single likeliest leg, then the best of those, so the legs are independent and the ticket's chance is the product of its legs. Every leg is a line DraftKings posts (as ESPN carries it) for a hitter cleared by the injury report, with DraftKings' price where ESPN shows one; a scratched leg is void and the rest ride. Tickets lock the moment any of their games starts. Fair odds are the price a ticket needs to break even; parlays carry a steep house edge — never stake what you cannot afford to lose.

Why it is kept out of the model
The largest batter-vs-pitcher sample all season is 13 plate appearances, and only about 520 pairs reach even 8. At that size the standard error on a hit rate is roughly .16, wider than the entire spread of true talent between major-league hitters. A 4-for-9 line is noise that looks like a trend, so it is shown with its sample size attached and deliberately kept out of the model.
