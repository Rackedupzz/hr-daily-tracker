How HOMER does his homework
  • Expected home runs. Every batted ball this season is binned by exit velocity and launch angle; the league's HR rate per bin turns a hitter's contact into the home runs it deserved.
  • Form & splits. Last-21-day xHR, the hitter's own record against today's pitcher hand, barrel rate, and the league platoon effect.
  • Pitchers. HR and xHR allowed per batter faced, fly-ball rate and recent form for the starter, and the bullpen he hands off to.
  • Park factor from HR/PA at the venue vs the home team's road games; weather from temperature and wind toward the hitter's pull field.
  • He learns what wins. The season is replayed week by week with his book frozen at each week's start. Weights are fitted only on earlier weeks and graded on the next one. Factors that don't improve those unseen-week predictions are dropped, and the learned weights are used only if they beat his original rule-of-thumb formula.
  • Grades are earned. A / B+ / B / C bands come from where a pick sits on the replayed board, and each shows how often picks in that band actually homered.
  • Games. A wOBA runs model (lineup vs the starter and bullpen it faces) and season run differential, weighted the same learned way.
  • Hit picks. Hit rate, expected hits from contact quality, strikeout and walk rates, and his own split, against the starter's hits allowed and strikeout rate, the bullpen and the park — learned and tested the same way.
  • HOMER'S PLAYS. 3- and 4-leg hit combos, one leg per game, each held to cashing three days in ten — plus two 5-pick parlays (five homers, five hits) that carry their own record. Every ticket is rebuilt on every replayed day to check that its quoted odds hold up.
  • Grows every day. His book refreshes with each data pull, and the whole season replay reruns daily, so new results reshape his weights and grades.
  • Selection rules. Six HR picks and six hit picks, one per game, 80+ PA, no injury notes, and only hitters in the posted lineup. Picks and plays in started games are locked.

💰HOMER’S PLAYS

Built leg by leg from HOMER's hit board, one leg per game so no single rainout or pitcher sinks the ticket. Every play here cleared the same bar in the replay — it had to cash on at least three days in ten — so what separates them is price, not whether they land. Parlays are still high-variance: stake accordingly and never bet more than you can afford to lose.

🎲 How the book works — and how HOMER counters
  • The juice compounds. Every leg carries the book's margin, and a parlay multiplies them. Four hit legs at a normal hold pay about 22% less than their true odds, which is why none of these tickets runs long.
  • No home run legs. HOMER's best home run bat cashes about one start in five, and a parlay can never beat its weakest leg — so a ticket carrying one cannot clear the cash bar these plays are held to. His home run board is below; play those straight.
  • The book leans on the season line. Props are priced mostly off a hitter's rate and name. HOMER prices the matchup: the starter, bullpen, park, weather, lineup slot and contact quality. "Built to pay" plays only take legs where that read clearly beats the season line.
  • One leg per game. Legs from the same game move together, and books reprice same-game parlays to take that edge back. Spreading legs keeps the math honest.
  • A scratched player voids his leg; the ticket rides on the rest at a smaller payout.
  • Price is estimated until live odds are wired in. If your book is shorter than the fair odds, pass or hunt a boost.
  • Size by how often it cashes, not by what it pays. These plays land between three and four days in ten, so flat stakes suit all of them — but even a 41% ticket goes quiet for a week or more, and the replay line on each card shows its worst run. Read that before you stake it.
